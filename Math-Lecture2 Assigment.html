Concept & Definition

A fraction shows part of a whole as numerator / denominator. To add or subtract, first rewrite the fractions over a common denominator (LCD). To multiply, multiply straight across. To divide, multiply by the reciprocal (flip the second fraction).

Q1 Basic
Evaluate: \( \dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{3} \)
1
LCD of 4 and 3 is 12.
2
Convert: \( \dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{3}{12},\ \ \dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{4}{12} \)
3
Add numerators: \( \dfrac{3}{12}+\dfrac{4}{12}=\dfrac{7}{12} \)
Answer \( \dfrac{7}{12} \)
Q2 Basic
Evaluate: \( \dfrac{3}{5} - \dfrac{1}{4} \)
1
LCD of 5 and 4 is 20.
2
Convert: \( \dfrac{3}{5}=\dfrac{12}{20},\ \ \dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{5}{20} \)
3
Subtract: \( \dfrac{12}{20}-\dfrac{5}{20}=\dfrac{7}{20} \)
Answer \( \dfrac{7}{20} \)
Q3 Basic
Evaluate: \( \dfrac{2}{3} \times \dfrac{3}{8} \)
1
Multiply numerators and denominators: \( \dfrac{2\times3}{3\times8}=\dfrac{6}{24} \)
2
Simplify by dividing by HCF = 6: \( \dfrac{6\div6}{24\div6}=\dfrac{1}{4} \)
Answer \( \dfrac{1}{4} \)
Q4 Intermediate
Evaluate: \( \dfrac{5}{6} \div \dfrac{2}{3} \)
1
Division = multiply by reciprocal: \( \dfrac{5}{6}\times\dfrac{3}{2} \)

3
Simplify (÷3): \( \dfrac{15}{12}=\dfrac{5}{4}=1\dfrac{1}{4} \)
Answer \( 1\dfrac{1}{4} \)
Q5 Intermediate
Evaluate: \( 1\dfrac{1}{2} + 2\dfrac{2}{3} \)
1
Convert to improper fractions: \( 1\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{3}{2},\ \ 2\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{8}{3} \)

LCD of 2 and 3 is 6: \( \dfrac{3}{2}=\dfrac{9}{6},\ \ \dfrac{8}{3}=\dfrac{16}{6} \)

4
Write as a mixed number: \( \dfrac{25}{6}=4\dfrac{1}{6} \)
Answer \( 4\dfrac{1}{6} \)
Q6 Intermediate
Evaluate: \( 3\dfrac{1}{4} - 1\dfrac{5}{6} \)
1
Convert to improper fractions: \( 3\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{13}{4},\ \ 1\dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{11}{6} \)

LCD of 4 and 6 is 12: \( \dfrac{13}{4}=\dfrac{39}{12},\ \ \dfrac{11}{6}=\dfrac{22}{12} \)
3
Subtract: \( \dfrac{39}{12}-\dfrac{22}{12}=\dfrac{17}{12} \)
4
Write as a mixed number: \( \dfrac{17}{12}=1\dfrac{5}{12} \)
Answer \( 1\dfrac{5}{12} \)
Q7 Intermediate
Evaluate: \( \dfrac{2}{5} \times 1\dfrac{1}{4} \div \dfrac{3}{10} \)
1
Convert mixed number: \( 1\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{5}{4} \)

Multiply first: \( \dfrac{2}{5}\times\dfrac{5}{4}=\dfrac{10}{20}=\dfrac{1}{2} \)

Divide by \( \dfrac{3}{10} \): multiply by reciprocal \( \dfrac{1}{2}\times\dfrac{10}{3} \)

Q8 Advanced
Evaluate: \( \left(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}\right) \div \left(\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{3}\right) \)
1
Add inside first bracket (LCD 6): \( \dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{3}{6}+\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{5}{6} \)
2

3

Q9 Advanced
Evaluate: \( 2\dfrac{1}{3} \times 1\dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{3}{4} \div \dfrac{1}{2} \)
1
Convert mixed numbers: \( 2\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{7}{3},\ \ 1\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{3}{2} \)
2
Multiply first: \( \dfrac{7}{3}\times\dfrac{3}{2}=\dfrac{21}{6}=\dfrac{7}{2} \)
3
Divide second part: \( \dfrac{3}{4}\div\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{3}{4}\times2=\dfrac{3}{2} \)
4
Subtract (LCD 2): \( \dfrac{7}{2}-\dfrac{3}{2}=\dfrac{4}{2}=2 \)
Answer \( 2 \)
Q10 Advanced
Evaluate: \( \left[\dfrac{3}{4} + \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\times\dfrac{2}{3}\right)\right] \div \left[\dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{1}{3}\right] \)
1
Inner multiplication: \( \dfrac{1}{2}\times\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{1}{3} \)
2
Add in first bracket (LCD 12): \( \dfrac{3}{4}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{9}{12}+\dfrac{4}{12}=\dfrac{13}{12} \)
3
Subtract in second bracket (LCD 6): \( \dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{3}{6}=\dfrac{1}{2} \)
4
Divide: \( \dfrac{13}{12}\div\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{13}{12}\times2=\dfrac{26}{12}=\dfrac{13}{6} \)
5
Write as a mixed number: \( \dfrac{13}{6}=2\dfrac{1}{6} \)
Answer \( 2\dfrac{1}{6} \)
Concept & Definition

The square root of a number n is the value that, multiplied by itself, gives n (√n × √n = n). The cube root is the value that, multiplied by itself three times, gives n. Perfect squares/cubes have whole-number roots.

Q1 Basic
Evaluate: \( \sqrt{49} \)
1
Find a number which, multiplied by itself, gives 49.
2
\( 7\times7=49 \)
Answer \( 7 \)
Q2 Basic

1
Find a number which, multiplied by itself, gives 121.
2
\( 11\times11=121 \)
Answer \( 11 \)

1
Find a number which, cubed, gives 8.
2
\( 2\times2\times2=8 \)
Answer \( 2 \)

Evaluate each root separately: \( \sqrt{144}=12,\ \ \sqrt{16}=4 \)
2
Add: \( 12+4=16 \)
Answer \( 16 \)
Q5 Intermediate
Evaluate: \( \sqrt[3]{64} - \sqrt[3]{27} \)
1
Evaluate each root: \( \sqrt[3]{64}=4,\ \ \sqrt[3]{27}=3 \)

Answer \( 1 \)

\( \sqrt{81}=9,\ \ \sqrt{9}=3 \)

\( \sqrt{200}=\sqrt{100}\times\sqrt{2}=10\sqrt{2} \)

Q9 Advanced

\( \sqrt[3]{250}=\sqrt[3]{125}\times\sqrt[3]{2}=5\sqrt[3]{2} \)

Concept & Definition

Multi-step fraction problems combine several operations. Work through them using the correct order of operations, find LCDs as needed, and isolate the unknown with inverse operations when solving an equation.

Q1 Basic
Find the LCD of \( \dfrac{1}{6} \) and \( \dfrac{1}{4} \), then add them.
1
Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18 … Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12 …
2
LCD = 12.
3
Convert: \( \dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{2}{12},\ \ \dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{3}{12} \)
4
Add: \( \dfrac{2}{12}+\dfrac{3}{12}=\dfrac{5}{12} \)
Answer \( \dfrac{5}{12} \)
Q2 Basic
Simplify \( \dfrac{8}{12} \) to its lowest terms.
1
Find the HCF of 8 and 12, which is 4.
2
Divide numerator and denominator by 4: \( \dfrac{8\div4}{12\div4}=\dfrac{2}{3} \)
Answer \( \dfrac{2}{3} \)
Q3 Basic
Convert \( \dfrac{7}{2} \) to a mixed number.
1
Divide 7 by 2: quotient 3, remainder 1.
2
Write as a mixed number: \( 3\dfrac{1}{2} \)
Answer \( 3\dfrac{1}{2} \)
Q4 Intermediate
Simplify the complex fraction \( \dfrac{\ \dfrac{1}{2}\ }{\ \dfrac{3}{4}\ } \)
1
A complex fraction means division: \( \dfrac{1}{2}\div\dfrac{3}{4} \)
2
Multiply by the reciprocal: \( \dfrac{1}{2}\times\dfrac{4}{3}=\dfrac{4}{6} \)
3
Simplify: \( \dfrac{4}{6}=\dfrac{2}{3} \)
Answer \( \dfrac{2}{3} \)
Q5 Intermediate
Evaluate using the LCD technique: \( \dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{1}{6} - \dfrac{1}{2} \)
1
LCD of 3, 6, 2 is 6.
2
Convert: \( \dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{4}{6},\ \ \dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{1}{6},\ \ \dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{3}{6} \)
3
Combine: \( \dfrac{4}{6}+\dfrac{1}{6}-\dfrac{3}{6}=\dfrac{2}{6} \)
4
Simplify: \( \dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{1}{3} \)
Answer \( \dfrac{1}{3} \)
Q6 Intermediate
Compare \( \dfrac{3}{5} \) and \( \dfrac{5}{8} \) using cross-multiplication.
1
Cross-multiply: \( 3\times8=24 \) and \( 5\times5=25 \)
2
Since \( 24 < 25 \), the fraction on the left is smaller.
Answer \( \dfrac{3}{5} < \dfrac{5}{8} \)
Q7 Intermediate
Simplify the complex fraction \( \dfrac{2+\dfrac{1}{3}}{1-\dfrac{1}{4}} \)
1
Simplify the numerator: \( 2+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{7}{3} \)
2
Simplify the denominator: \( 1-\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{3}{4} \)
3
Divide: \( \dfrac{7}{3}\div\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{7}{3}\times\dfrac{4}{3}=\dfrac{28}{9} \)
4
Write as a mixed number: \( \dfrac{28}{9}=3\dfrac{1}{9} \)
Answer \( 3\dfrac{1}{9} \)
Q8 Advanced
Solve for \( x \): \( \dfrac{x}{4} + \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{5}{6} \)
1
Isolate the term with \( x \): \( \dfrac{x}{4} = \dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{1}{3} \)
2
LCD of 6 and 3 is 6: \( \dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{3}{6}=\dfrac{1}{2} \)
3
So \( \dfrac{x}{4}=\dfrac{1}{2} \). Multiply both sides by 4: \( x=2 \)
Answer \( x = 2 \)
Q9 Advanced
Simplify the nested fraction \( \dfrac{1}{1+\dfrac{1}{1+\dfrac{1}{2}}} \)
1
Simplify innermost part: \( 1+\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{3}{2} \)
2
Next layer: \( 1+\dfrac{1}{\ \dfrac{3}{2}\ }=1+\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{5}{3} \)
3
Final layer: \( \dfrac{1}{\ \dfrac{5}{3}\ }=\dfrac{3}{5} \)
Answer \( \dfrac{3}{5} \)
Q10 Advanced
Evaluate: \( \left(\dfrac{3}{4} \div \dfrac{1}{8}\right) - \left(\dfrac{2}{3} \times \dfrac{3}{5}\right) \)
1
First bracket: \( \dfrac{3}{4}\div\dfrac{1}{8}=\dfrac{3}{4}\times8=6 \)
2
Second bracket: \( \dfrac{2}{3}\times\dfrac{3}{5}=\dfrac{6}{15}=\dfrac{2}{5} \)
3
Subtract (LCD 5): \( 6-\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{30}{5}-\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{28}{5} \)
4
Write as a mixed number: \( \dfrac{28}{5}=5\dfrac{3}{5} \)
Answer \( 5\dfrac{3}{5} \)
Concept & Definition

Real numbers obey consistent rules: Commutative (order doesn't matter for + / ×), Associative (grouping doesn't matter), Distributive (a(b+c) = ab+ac), Identity (adding 0 or × by 1 changes nothing), and Inverse (a number plus its opposite is 0; a number times its reciprocal is 1).

Q1 Basic
Name the property shown: \( 5+7=7+5 \)
1
The order of the numbers is switched, but the sum stays the same.
2
This is the Commutative Property of Addition.
Answer \( \text{Commutative Property of Addition} \)
Q2 Basic
Name the property shown: \( 3\times(4\times5)=(3\times4)\times5 \)
1
The grouping of the numbers changes, but the product stays the same.
2
This is the Associative Property of Multiplication.
Answer \( \text{Associative Property of Multiplication} \)
Q3 Basic
Name the property shown: \( 9+0=9 \)
1
Adding 0 does not change the value.
2
This is the Additive Identity Property.
Answer \( \text{Additive Identity} \)
Q4 Intermediate
Use the distributive property to expand \( 4(x+3) \)
1
Distribute the 4 to each term inside the bracket: \( 4\times x + 4\times3 \)
2
Simplify: \( 4x+12 \)
Answer \( 4x+12 \)
Q5 Intermediate
Name the property shown: \( 6\times1=6 \)
1
Multiplying by 1 does not change the value.
2
This is the Multiplicative Identity Property.
Answer \( \text{Multiplicative Identity} \)
Q6 Intermediate
Find the additive inverse of \( -8 \).
1
The additive inverse is the number that, added to the original, gives 0.
2
\( -8 + 8 = 0 \)
Answer \( 8 \)
Q7 Intermediate
Find the multiplicative inverse (reciprocal) of \( \dfrac{3}{5} \).
1
The multiplicative inverse is the number that, multiplied by the original, gives 1.
2
\( \dfrac{3}{5}\times\dfrac{5}{3}=1 \)
Answer \( \dfrac{5}{3} \)
Q8 Advanced

1

\( =14x - 21 + 5 \)

Concept & Definition

A factor of a number divides it exactly, with no remainder. Prime factorization breaks a number into a product of primes. The total count of factors comes from the exponents in that factorization: multiply (exponent + 1) for every prime.

Q1 Basic
List all the factors of 12.
1
Test each whole number from 1 up to 12 to see if it divides evenly.
2
\( 1,2,3,4,6,12 \) all divide 12 exactly.
Answer \( 1,2,3,4,6,12 \)
Q2 Basic
Is 7 a factor of 28?
1
Divide: \( 28\div7=4 \), a whole number.
2
Since it divides exactly, yes, 7 is a factor of 28.
Answer \( \text{Yes} \)
Q3 Basic
List all the factors of 20.
1
Test each whole number up to 20.
2
\( 1,2,4,5,10,20 \) all divide 20 exactly.
Answer \( 1,2,4,5,10,20 \)
Q4 Intermediate
Find the greatest factor of 36, other than 36 itself.
1
List the factors of 36: \( 1,2,3,4,6,9,12,18,36 \)
2
The largest factor smaller than 36 is 18.
Answer \( 18 \)
Q5 Intermediate
Find the common factors of 18 and 24.
1
Factors of 18: \( 1,2,3,6,9,18 \)
2
Factors of 24: \( 1,2,3,4,6,8,12,24 \)
3
Common factors: \( 1,2,3,6 \)
Answer \( 1,2,3,6 \)
Q6 Intermediate
Write the prime factorization of 60.
1
Divide by the smallest prime repeatedly: \( 60=2\times30=2\times2\times15=2\times2\times3\times5 \)
2
So \( 60=2^2\times3\times5 \)
Answer \( 2^2\times3\times5 \)
Q7 Intermediate
How many factors does 28 have?
1
List them: \( 1,2,4,7,14,28 \)
2
Count them: there are 6 factors.

Q8 Advanced
Find the number of factors of 72 using prime factorization.
1
Prime factorize: \( 72=2^3\times3^2 \)
2
Number of factors \( =(3+1)(2+1)=4\times3=12 \)
Answer \( 12 \)
Q9 Advanced
Find the Greatest Common Factor (GCF) of 48 and 60 using prime factorization.
1
Prime factorize both: \( 48=2^4\times3,\ \ 60=2^2\times3\times5 \)

Take the lowest power of each common prime: \( 2^2\times3 \)

Concept & Definition

A multiple of a number is that number times any whole number (n×1, n×2, n×3 …). Multiples are generated by repeated addition and continue forever in both size and count.

Concept & Definition

The LCM of two or more numbers is the smallest number that is a multiple of every one of them. Find it via prime factorization (take the highest power of each prime that appears) or the division-ladder method.

Q1 Basic
Find the LCM of 4 and 6.
1
Multiples of 4: 4,8,12,16
2
Multiples of 6: 6,12,18
3
Smallest common multiple: 12.
Answer \( 12 \)
Q2 Basic
Find the LCM of 5 and 10.
1
Since 10 is already a multiple of 5, the LCM is 10.
Answer \( 10 \)
Q3 Basic
Find the LCM of 3 and 7.
1
3 and 7 share no common factors other than 1 (co-prime).
2
LCM \( =3\times7=21 \)
Answer \( 21 \)
Q4 Intermediate
Find the LCM of 12 and 18 using prime factorization.
1
Prime factorize: \( 12=2^2\times3,\ \ 18=2\times3^2 \)
2
Take the highest power of each prime: \( 2^2\times3^2 \)
3
\( =4\times9=36 \)
Answer \( 36 \)
Q5 Intermediate
Find the LCM of 8, 12, and 15 using prime factorization.
1
Prime factorize: \( 8=2^3,\ \ 12=2^2\times3,\ \ 15=3\times5 \)
2
Take the highest power of each prime: \( 2^3\times3\times5 \)
3
\( =8\times3\times5=120 \)
Answer \( 120 \)
Q6 Intermediate
Find the LCM of 24 and 36 using the division ladder method.
1
Divide both by common primes step by step:
2
\( 2\ |\ 24,36 \to 12,18 \)
3
\( 2\ |\ 12,18 \to 6,9 \)
4
\( 3\ |\ 6,9 \to 2,3 \)
5
Multiply divisors and remaining numbers: \( 2\times2\times3\times2\times3=72 \)
Answer \( 72 \)
Q7 Intermediate
Find the LCM of 9 and 6 using the relation \( \text{LCM}\times\text{HCF}=\text{product of numbers} \)
1
Find the HCF of 9 and 6 first: \( \text{HCF}=3 \)
2
Use the relation: \( \text{LCM}=\dfrac{9\times6}{3} \)
3
\( =\dfrac{54}{3}=18 \)
Answer \( 18 \)
Q8 Advanced
Find the LCM of 18, 24, and 30 using prime factorization.
1
Prime factorize: \( 18=2\times3^2,\ \ 24=2^3\times3,\ \ 30=2\times3\times5 \)
2
Take the highest power of each prime: \( 2^3\times3^2\times5 \)
3
\( =8\times9\times5=360 \)
Answer \( 360 \)
Q9 Advanced
Two runners complete one lap of a track in 12 minutes and 18 minutes respectively. If they start together, after how many minutes will they next be together at the starting point?
1
This requires the LCM of 12 and 18.
2
\( 12=2^2\times3,\ \ 18=2\times3^2 \Rightarrow \text{LCM}=2^2\times3^2=36 \)
3
They will meet again after 36 minutes.
Answer \( 36\ \text{minutes} \)
Q10 Advanced
Find the smallest number exactly divisible by 15, 20, and 25.
1
This is asking for the LCM of 15, 20, 25.
2
Prime factorize: \( 15=3\times5,\ \ 20=2^2\times5,\ \ 25=5^2 \)
3
Take the highest power of each prime: \( 2^2\times3\times5^2 \)
4
\( =4\times3\times25=300 \)
Answer \( 300 \)
Concept & Definition

The HCF (or GCD) of two or more numbers is the largest number that divides all of them exactly. Find it via prime factorization (shared primes at their lowest powers) or the Euclidean algorithm (repeated division).

Q1 Basic
Find the HCF of 8 and 12.
1
Factors of 8: 1,2,4,8
2
Factors of 12: 1,2,3,4,6,12
3
Greatest common factor: 4.
Answer \( 4 \)
Q2 Basic
Find the HCF of 15 and 25.
1
Factors of 15: 1,3,5,15
2
Factors of 25: 1,5,25
3
Greatest common factor: 5.
Answer \( 5 \)
Q3 Basic
Find the HCF of 9 and 27.
1
Since 27 is a multiple of 9, the HCF is 9.
Answer \( 9 \)
Q4 Intermediate
Find the HCF of 24 and 36 using prime factorization.
1
Prime factorize: \( 24=2^3\times3,\ \ 36=2^2\times3^2 \)
2
Take the lowest power of each common prime: \( 2^2\times3 \)
3

Concept & Definition

An exponent shows repeated multiplication: an = a×a×…×a (n times). Key rules — product aᵐ·aⁿ=aᵐ⁺ⁿ, quotient aᵐ÷aⁿ=aᵐ⁻ⁿ, power of a power (aᵐ)ⁿ=aᵐⁿ, zero exponent a⁰=1, and negative exponent a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ.
